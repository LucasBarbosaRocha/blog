This is my space to write about everything I've run into in computing.

That covers the command I spent an afternoon looking for, the cluster that wouldn't boot, the LaTeX float
that refused to stay put, the judge problem that finally got accepted, and the papers that came out of years of
research. If I had to figure it out once, it goes here, so neither of us has to figure it out again.

I'm **Lucas Barbosa Carinhanha Rocha**, a backend developer from Campo Grande, Brazil, with a PhD in Computer Science.
My research was on algorithms and graphs, especially de Bruijn graphs, applied to bioinformatics.
Along the way I set up Linux HPC clusters, competed in programming contests and worked as a teaching assistant.
These days I mostly build Java services that talk to each other.

*Capybara Informs* is the English version of the blog's original name, *Capivara Informações*,
from its first life in 2016, when it was written in Portuguese. The capybara is calm, gets along with everyone
and is very Brazilian, which is a decent goal for a blog. The older posts have been translated, revised and, where they've aged
badly, marked as legacy.

Expect a bit of everything: Linux, HPC, programming, LaTeX, research, and whatever I'm fiddling with next.
Usually with a cup of tereré nearby.
